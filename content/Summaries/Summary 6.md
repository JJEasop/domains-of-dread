# Summary 6

Vistani Encampment Reception and Cultural Observations
The party entered a Vistani camp arranged in a circle of wagons (vardos), with tents set outside the circle. The encampment showed established yet mobile infrastructure: a breakdown clay oven for bread, drying racks for herbs, laundry lines, and craft stations. Dinner featured spiced wild game stew (deer), a bird-based rice pilaf, and warm bread; hospitality was generous and communal, with rugs around a central fire inviting conversation.

Camp life appeared relaxed and content: elders smoked pipes, craftsmen whittled and wove baskets, and groups played dice and classic board games. Children were lively, curious, and distinctly unlike the “dead-eyed” townsfolk of Barovia. A notable pipe’s wood (recognized as the same as Madame Eva’s from night one) was described as fine-grained, extremely dark with a reddish tint and metallic glimmer. Instruments included violins, a viola, lutes, and a notably modern-style acoustic guitar; no bagpipes were present, though a chanter-like drone was heard.

Jordi (spelled J-O-R-D-I), the caravan’s male leader/captain, greeted the party and emphasized Vistani hospitality and storytelling tradition. On lore, he confirmed the nightly ghost procession from Barovia ascends past the waterfall toward Castle Ravenloft. He noted his sister is the caravan’s elder and would arrive later. Party members integrated respectfully—observing customs, engaging elders, and entertaining curious children (including a fascinated toddler who fixated on Lucia’s holy symbol).


Campfire Tales and Regional Lore Exposition
Storyteller Marcella related a chain of lore-rich tales central to Vistani cosmology and local history. First, the cautionary saga of Gabriella: the daughter of a proud woman once promised as Rauni (elder). Gabriella’s mother, who possessed the sight, warned her against bearing children. During a full moon herb-gathering, Gabriella tripped her own mother to escape a beast; the Mists rejected Gabriella’s selfishness and displaced her into the realm of a Lord who hunted young brides monthly. Gabriella froze the Lord with her gaze and slew him with a silver dagger, claiming his palace and status as mortu (a Vistani who no longer travels). She later bore a son, Malocchio, who inherited the sight—unusual and forbidden for a boy—and became the Dukkar, a prophesied enemy of the Vistani. Gabriella trained him to hunt their people; Malocchio killed her first, then continued hunting the Vistani relentlessly.

The Sasha tale followed: Petia (Vistani) and Elena (burgomaster’s daughter) fell in love; Elena’s father sentenced Petia to the gallows. A “golden-faced man,” an elf who had previously died, plucked Petia from the gallows and brought him to Petia’s mother, who read his fortune and spurred the caravan to avoid Barovia for two generations. Elena later married a baker; their son, Sasha, survived a house fire (the burgomaster’s home burned in the night as punishment from the Count for cheating taxes). Father Martin, a priest, raised Sasha and taught him to hunt creatures of the night. Sasha, his friend Liesel, and the golden-faced elf sought to infiltrate the Lord’s castle; Sasha found treasures in the crypt to fight the dark creatures. He believed he had killed them but learned later he had not. Sasha eventually became a priest, teaching others to hunt the undead.

Finally, Marcella recited the Vistani migration lament: Vistan (a conqueror) marched west with great legions, supported by the Vistani who forged weapons, shoed horses, and healed wounds. Defeated by “warriors of a dark god,” his legions were enslaved. The Vistani, forbidden to face East by the dark ones, followed Caldresh, who claimed the world was round and urged them to travel west to find their “home forge.” They ultimately concluded Caldresh lied and now believe the world is flat, and that they will never see their homelands again.

These tales cemented terms: Rauni (elder woman/caravan leader), mortu (Vistani settled, no longer traveling), Dukkar (a Vistani born with the sight who hunts Vistani), and affirmed how the Mists and Fate bind choices and consequences across generations.


Prophetic Tarokka Card Divination with Madame Vadoma
Madame Vadoma, the caravan elder and sister to Eva (the “Auntie” met earlier), invited the core group (the four PCs and Ireena) to a private fortune-telling in a dedicated work tent. The setup featured a covered orb, oils and herbs, a bowl of small bones, and a lavish hand-painted, gilded deck. Vadoma asked each to cut and draw one card, then laid a five-card cross for the group.

Individual draws:

* Ireena: Eight of Pentacles (The Necromancer) — “In your eyes, I see a woman hanging over a roaring fire. Find her, and you will find what you seek.”
* Michael: Eight of Coins (The Tax Collector) — “My people have what you seek. A missing child holds the key.”
* Pip: Five of Coins (The Guild Member) — “I see a dark room full of bottles. It is the tomb of a guild member. You will find treasure there.”
* Lucia: Six of Swords (The Sorcerer) — “Find the mad dog’s crypt. Your treasure lies within, beneath blackened bones.”
* Linnorden: The Tempter — “I see another child… Perhaps the same. You must hurry. Her fate hangs in the balance. Do not lose her in the water.” (Vadoma emphasized this as a warning-command.)


Group five-card cross:

* Past — Six of Glyphs (The Anarchist): “Bones everywhere: wall, chandelier, and table—remains of long-forgotten enemies.”
* Present — Seven of Pentacles (The Illusionist): “A man not what he seems comes for you in a wagon, both like and unlike a bardo. Therein lies great treasure.”
* Future — Eight of Glyphs (The Bishop): “A secret waits for you in a pile of treasure beyond golden doors.”
* Treachery — Six of Pentacles (The Evoker): “Search for the crypts of a great wizard. His staff is the key.”
* Fate — Two of Pentacles (The Diviner): “Look for the one who sees all. She holds the key to your fate.”


Vadoma drew an additional companion card:

* Companion — Five of Pentacles (The Elementalist): “There is a place where sickness and madness are bred, where children once cried. This person knows that place. They will lead you there.”
* Companion 2 — The Innocent.


Vadoma clarified familial relations: Eva is the elder sister previously encountered, and she warned, “I pray you never meet Marushka.” She cautioned that “Not all of us are of the same mind. We all serve,” and declined to interpret the readings further, urging the party to discover meaning through action. Linnorden arranged to discuss divinatory craft with Vadoma while others conduct trade the next morning.


Caravan Logistics, Spell Scribing, and Morning Negotiations
Overnight, the group took a long rest and rolled dreams/nightmares; one vivid nightmare involved standing on the deck of a sinking galley with loved ones bound to the masts. In the morning, Linnorden successfully scribed Detect Thoughts (2nd level) and Obscuring Mist (1st level) into their spellbook after successful checks.

Jordi offered one spare vardo and a covered wagon; two draft horses were also available. The initial barter pile included a tent, torches (including an ever-burning torch), a climber’s kit, three healing potions, two pounds of nails, and wine bottles. The silver jewelry box was refused and returned. The bargaining, conducted with Vistani theatrics (accusations of “beggaring” and the counter of “Are you selling me glue?”), culminated after Pip produced an unopened keg of Longbottom leaf (Southfarthing pipeweed). The caravan agreed to the vardo, covered wagon (with a fitted cover), and two draft horses in exchange for wine, tent, torches, climber’s kit, nails, and the pipeweed keg; the party retained their three potion vials. A thorough search of the vardo (DC 22) uncovered a hidden book under a bench seat.

The negotiation was flavored by the caravan’s use of a charming toddler (“Grandpa, don’t be mean”) and by Jordi’s boyish mischief. The deal recognized cultural norms of Vistani bargaining and concluded amicably.
